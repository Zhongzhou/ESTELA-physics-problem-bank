# Problem review

One problem for each of the four categories, each asking about position 1. The positive radial direction is toward the center of the circle. On the side-view figures, position 1 is the top, position 2 is the right-hand side, and position 3 is the bottom. On the top-view figures, position 1 is the leftmost point, position 2 is the point at the top of the figure, and position 3 is the rightmost point.

## 1. Vertical circle on a string

![Bucket of water, side view, positions 1, 2, and 3](diagrams/01-bucket-of-water.png)

A gardener swings a small bucket of water, tied to a rope, through a full vertical circle. The rope stays taut and the water stays in the bucket during the entire circle, as shown in the figure. The figure is a side view of the path that shows three positions of the bucket during the circle. The bucket is at the top of the path at position 1, at the right-hand side of the path at position 2, and at the bottom of the path at position 3.

Take the positive direction along the radius to be toward the center of the circle. Which of the following equations correctly expresses Newton's second law for the bucket of water **along the radial direction** at **position 1** (top of the path)?

- $T$ is the magnitude of the tension in the rope.
- $m$ is the mass of the bucket of water.
- $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.
- $a_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration of the bucket of water.
- $F_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal force on the bucket.

A. $T + mg = m a_c$
B. $T - mg = m a_c$
C. $T = m a_c$
D. $T + mg + F_c = m a_c$
E. $T - mg + F_c = m a_c$
F. $T + F_c = m a_c$

**Correct response:** A. $T + mg = m a_c$

**Feedback:** At the top, the rope pulls toward the center and the weight also points toward the center. Both contributions are positive, so $T + mg = m a_c$. The centripetal force is already that net inward force, so it is not added again.

## 2. Vertical circle on a frictionless track

![Toy car, side view, positions 1, 2, and 3](diagrams/03-toy-car.png)

A toy car moves on the inside of a smooth, frictionless track in the shape of a vertical loop. The toy car does not leave the track during the entire circle, as shown in the figure. The figure is a side view of the path that shows three positions of the toy car during the circle. The toy car is at the top of the path at position 1, at the right-hand side of the path at position 2, and at the bottom of the path at position 3.

Take the positive direction along the radius to be toward the center of the circle. Which of the following equations correctly expresses Newton's second law for the toy car **along the radial direction** at **position 1** (top of the path)?

- $N$ is the magnitude of the normal force exerted by the track on the toy car.
- $m$ is the mass of the toy car.
- $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.
- $a_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration of the toy car.
- $F_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal force on the toy car.

A. $N + mg = m a_c$
B. $N - mg = m a_c$
C. $N = m a_c$
D. $N + mg + F_c = m a_c$
E. $N - mg + F_c = m a_c$
F. $N + F_c = m a_c$

**Correct response:** A. $N + mg = m a_c$

**Feedback:** The toy car is on the inside of the loop, so at the top the track pushes the car toward the center. The weight also points toward the center. Both contributions are positive, so $N + mg = m a_c$. The centripetal force is already that net inward force, so it is not added again.

## 3. Horizontal circle on a string

![Model airplane, top view, wind left to right](diagrams/05-model-airplane.png)

A model airplane flies in a horizontal circle at the end of a horizontal tether. The tether stays taut during the entire circle, as shown in the figure. A constant wind blows the airplane from left to right during the entire circle. The airplane's weight is balanced by lift from the air. The figure is a top view of the path that shows three positions of the airplane during the circle. The airplane is at the leftmost point of the path at position 1, at the point at the top of the figure at position 2, and at the rightmost point of the path at position 3.

Take the positive direction along the radius to be toward the center of the circle. Which of the following equations correctly expresses Newton's second law for the airplane **along the radial direction** at **position 1** (leftmost point of the path)?

- $T$ is the magnitude of the tension in the tether.
- $F_w$ is the magnitude of the force exerted by the wind on the airplane.
- $m$ is the mass of the airplane.
- $a_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration of the airplane.
- $F_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal force on the airplane.

A. $T + F_w = m a_c$
B. $T - F_w = m a_c$
C. $T = m a_c$
D. $T + F_w + F_c = m a_c$
E. $T - F_w + F_c = m a_c$
F. $T + F_c = m a_c$

**Correct response:** A. $T + F_w = m a_c$

**Feedback:** Lift balances the airplane's weight, so both forces are vertical and drop out of the radial equation. At the leftmost point the center is to the right, and the wind also blows to the right. The tether and the wind both point toward the center, so $T + F_w = m a_c$. The centripetal force is already that net inward force, so it is not added again.

## 4. Horizontal circle on a flat road

![Car, top view, wind left to right](diagrams/09-car.png)

A car drives at constant speed around a flat, unbanked circular track. The engine provides only tangential forces to overcome resistance. A constant wind blows the car from left to right during the entire circle. The figure is a top view of the path that shows three positions of the car during the circle. The car is at the leftmost point of the path at position 1, at the point at the top of the figure at position 2, and at the rightmost point of the path at position 3.

Take the positive direction along the radius to be toward the center of the circle. Which of the following equations correctly expresses Newton's second law for the car **along the radial direction** at **position 1** (leftmost point of the path)?

- $f_s$ is the radial component of the static friction force between the tires of the car and the track. It is positive toward the center and negative away from the center.
- $F_w$ is the magnitude of the force exerted by the wind on the car.
- $m$ is the mass of the car.
- $a_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration of the car.
- $F_c$ is the magnitude of the centripetal force on the car.

A. $f_s + F_w = m a_c$
B. $f_s - F_w = m a_c$
C. $f_s = m a_c$
D. $f_s + F_w + F_c = m a_c$
E. $f_s - F_w + F_c = m a_c$
F. $f_s + F_c = m a_c$

**Correct response:** A. $f_s + F_w = m a_c$

**Feedback:** The engine's force is tangential, so it has no radial component. At the leftmost point the center is to the right, and the wind also blows to the right. With $f_s$ positive toward the center, $f_s + F_w = m a_c$. The centripetal force is already that net inward force, so it is not added again.
