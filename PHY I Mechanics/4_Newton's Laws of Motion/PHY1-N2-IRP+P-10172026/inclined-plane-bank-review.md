# Newton’s Second Law on Rough Inclines: complete bank review

The selected grid contains 24 conceptual multiple-choice questions. Every question has six choices. General feedback includes the free-body diagram of A, a solid velocity arrow, a dashed acceleration arrow, a bordered force-component table, and the correct vector equation. Feedback for each choice is listed separately.

Question figures show only geometry and coordinate axes. Falling-right inclines include downhill motion only. Feedback diagrams place the axes on A and mark theta between negative y and gravity.

## 1. U0: Wooden crate — uphill, speeding up

In a warehouse demonstration, a wooden crate A of mass \(m_A\) slides on a rough loading ramp that rises to the right at an angle \(\theta\) above the horizontal. A taut, massless, inextensible rope runs parallel to the ramp from the crate, passes over a fixed, frictionless, massless pulley at the upper right end, and then runs vertically downward to a freely hanging sandbag B of mass \(m_B\).

The coefficient of kinetic friction between the crate and the ramp is \(\mu_k\). **At the instant shown, the crate is moving up the incline and speeding up.** It remains in contact with the ramp.

![Object A, a wooden crate, is on the left on a rough incline sloping upward to the right. A rope runs parallel to the incline to a fixed pulley at the upper right end, then vertically down to object B, a freely hanging sandbag, on the right. The angle theta is marked between the surface and a horizontal reference. The axes originate on A: positive x points right along the incline and positive y points perpendicular to and outward from the surface.](media/U0.png)

Let \(T\) be the rope-tension magnitude, \(N\) the normal-force magnitude on A, \(g\) the magnitude of gravitational acceleration, and \(a_A>0\) the magnitude of A’s acceleration. The unit vectors \(\hat{\imath}\) and \(\hat{\jmath}\) point along the positive \(x\) and \(y\) axes shown in the figure.

**Which equation correctly expresses Newton’s second law for object A in the coordinate system shown?**

**A.**

\[
\left(T-m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\hat{\imath}
\]

**B.**

\[
\left(T-m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=-m_A a_A\hat{\imath}
\]

**C.**

\[
\left(T-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\hat{\imath}
\]

**D.**

\[
\left(T-m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\left(\cos\theta\,\hat{\imath}+\sin\theta\,\hat{\jmath}\right)
\]

**E.**

\[
\left(T+m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\hat{\imath}
\]

**F.**

\[
\left(T-m_A g\sin\theta+\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\hat{\imath}
\]

### General feedback

**Instructor key: A.**

![Free-body diagram of isolated object A. Tension points uphill toward the right, the normal force is perpendicular to and outward from the incline, weight points vertically down, and kinetic friction points downhill, opposite the motion. Above A, a solid velocity arrow points uphill toward the right; a dashed acceleration arrow points uphill toward the right. The coordinate axes originate on A: positive x points right along the incline and positive y points perpendicular and outward. A dotted guide extends in the negative y direction. The acute angle theta is marked between this negative y direction and the vertically downward weight.](media/fbd-U-uphill-speeding-up.png)

*Solid arrow: velocity. Dashed arrow: acceleration. Arrow lengths are schematic.*

A moves uphill, along \(+x\), and is speeding up. Its acceleration therefore points uphill, along \(+x\). Let \(v_A>0\) denote its speed.

\[\vec{v}_A=v_A\hat{\imath},\qquad\vec{a}_A=a_A\hat{\imath}.\]

Tension points toward the pulley, along \(+x\). Gravity’s parallel component points downhill, along \(-x\). Kinetic friction opposes the velocity and points along \(-x\). The normal force points along \(+y\), while gravity’s perpendicular component points along \(-y\). The angle between gravity and \(-y\) is \(\theta\), giving \(F_{g,y}=-m_A g\cos\theta\).

The straight surface constrains the perpendicular acceleration to zero: \(a_y=0\).

\[N-m_A g\cos\theta=m_A a_y=0.\]

\[\left(T-m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N\right)\hat{\imath}+\left(N-m_A g\cos\theta\right)\hat{\jmath}=m_A a_A\hat{\imath}\]

\[T-m_A g\sin\theta-\mu_k N=m_A a_A,\qquad N-m_A g\cos\theta=0.\]

### Feedback for each choice

| Choice | Feedback |
|---|---|
| A | Correct. Tension is along \(+x\), parallel gravity is along \(-x\), friction is along \(-x\), and acceleration is along \(+x\). |
| B | The acceleration sign is reversed. A is moving up the incline and speeding up, so \(\vec{a}_A=a_A\hat{\imath}\). |
| C | The parallel component of gravity is missing. Its \(x\) component is \(-m_A g\sin\theta\). |
| D | The acceleration has been projected using the incline angle even though the axes are already aligned with the incline. A stays on the straight surface, so \(a_y=0\). |
| E | The parallel component of gravity has the wrong sign. Gravity points downhill, along \(-x\), giving \(F_{g,x}=-m_A g\sin\theta\). |
| F | The friction sign is reversed. Kinetic friction opposes A’s velocity, so it points along \(-x\), with \(f_{k,x}=-\mu_k N\). |
